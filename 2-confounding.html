The study as executed

Where the design departs from "3 batches × 3 replicates"

Five consequences fall out of the layout table. Each one has to be dealt with explicitly in the manuscript, because each one is visible to a reviewer who reads the supplementary design table.

Operator is completely aliased with kit

OperatorKits preparedException
CDMEVO, WMEVO cfDNA batch 1 in the first run was prepared by VK.
VKKAPA15, KAPAON, KAPAON-COV, IL, TWIST

There is no contrast in this dataset from which an operator effect can be estimated, and no model (mixed, fixed or otherwise) can adjust for it. Your supervisor's framing, "this is OK if we assume there is no difference between the operators", is exactly right, and the important consequence is that the assumption is untestable internally.

Recommended handling

Do not attempt to test it with these data. The fallback plan (VK vs CDM over time in the CG production environment) is the correct evidence, and it should go into the supplement pre-emptively, not held back until a reviewer asks. A limitation that you raise and answer yourself reads as rigour; the same limitation found by a reviewer reads as an oversight.

"Batch" does not mean the same thing for every kit

For most kits the three batches are three separate days. For WM in gDNA, FFPE 25 ng and FFPE 150 ng, all three batches were prepared on 2025-04-02 on three plates (dev250402.1 / .2 / .3). For WM cfDNA, batches 1 and 2 share a day. For EVO cfDNA, batches 2 and 3 share a day, in both runs.

This is a direct, quantitative bias: WM's between-batch variance measures plate-to-plate variation within one day, everyone else's measures day-to-day variation.

A claim you cannot make

Any statement that WM is the most reproducible kit is not supported. TWIST (1.9 %) and IL (2.3 %) achieved lower or equal batch-to-batch CV across three genuinely separate days, which is the harder test. State this plainly rather than leave it for a reviewer to find.

Kit is confounded with calendar time

Preparations span 2024-11-12 to 2025-04-14, five months, from a single pooled DNA stock per matrix. Aliquot storage and freeze-thaw effects are therefore aliased with kit. The only within-day kit contrasts available anywhere in the study are:

  • KAPA15 vs KAPAON, in all three sample types.
  • KAPAON vs KAPAON-COV, in FFPE only.

Those two contrasts are the study's internal controls and are worth naming as such in the Discussion.

Make this argument explicitly

The largest single effect in the whole study (a roughly 5-fold gDNA difference) sits in the one place where fragmentation is confounded with prep week. But the FFPE arms share prep days between the two fragmentation methods and reproduce the same direction and a similar magnitude. Use FFPE as the internal replication that de-confounds the gDNA result. The argument is available; a statistical reviewer will flag the gDNA comparison if you do not make it.

EVO's between-batch variance is therefore partly attributable to inconsistent adapter titration rather than to the chemistry. Across kits the ratio also differs systematically (cfDNA: EVO and WM at 275, KAPA at 220, TWIST at 110), so part of every kit contrast is also an adapter-concentration contrast.

The draft already flags "Include adapter:insert ratio" as a Methods placeholder. It needs to appear as a reported covariate in Table 1, as a stated limitation in the Discussion, and Fig S3 (adapter–insert ratio) should be cited at the point where EVO's batch spread is discussed.
