Compliance

Alignment with Karolinska Institutet research standards

The statistical review is consistent with, and in places strengthens, KI's expectations on good research practice. What it does not cover is the formal compliance layer: ethics and legal basis, data management, archiving, authorship, conflicts of interest and open access. Those gaps are listed here with the change each one implies.

Read this page as a prompt list, not as legal advice

This page was written without access to the KI staff portal, so every named rule below should be verified against the current version on staff.ki.se before you rely on it, and the registration numbers filled in. Your department (MEB) and KI's research data support and legal office are the authoritative sources. Treat each item as "confirm this and record the answer", not as a settled statement of the rule.

Where the review already aligns

Three of the changes recommended elsewhere on this site are not merely statistical improvements. They are good-research-practice requirements under the Swedish Research Council's God forskningssed and the ALLEA European Code of Conduct for Research Integrity, both of which KI adheres to.

PrincipleWhat the review does
Analyses must match the design; results must not be overstated Replaces a pseudoreplicated test with a batch-level model, and reports that FFPE 25 ng supports no pairwise claim rather than quoting uncorrected p-values.
Selective reporting and post-hoc data exclusion are integrity issues Separates the four capture failures, which are a legitimate quality exclusion, from three EVO libraries still dropped by name, and requires the grepl()-hidden EVO run choice and the capture-failure threshold to be stated in the Methods.
Limitations must be stated by the authors, not discovered by reviewers Puts operator aliasing, same-day batches, unequal adapter ratios and the single DNA pool into the Methods and Limitations text.
Fabrication and falsification Refuses every number from the two AI-generated documents in manuscript/ and recomputes from the raw data.

Nothing on this site conflicts with KI standards. The issue is coverage, not correctness.

Ethics, consent and legal basis

The study uses leftover plasma from ProBio participants, blood genomic DNA from two donors, and FFPE punches from nine patient blocks. The draft Methods do not state the permits covering that material.

The repository does hold ethics documentation, in documentation/ethical/

Three sets of decisions from the Swedish Ethical Review Authority are filed there, in Swedish, and the diary numbers are readable from the filenames:

  • ICAP, application and approval under 2009/780-31, with ten subsequent amendments (kompletteringar) running 2009/1357-32 through 2018/11615-32.
  • ICAP, localised ctDNA, submitted 2018-10-05.
  • STHLM3-MR, application June 2017 and approvals August 2017, with a ctDNA amendment approved 2018-12-28. Includes the participant information sheet.

Do not assume any of these covers this study. They date from 2017 to 2018 and belong to the ICAP and STHLM3-MR cohorts, whereas the material here is described as coming from ProBio. The task is to establish which permit the plasma, the donor blood and the FFPE blocks actually sit under, and whether methods development is within its scope. But the starting point is in the repository, not somewhere else.

Research data management

  • No data management plan exists in this project. KI expects a DMP for research projects, and funders generally require one. documentation/ holds the analysis plan and codebook templates but no DMP. Write one, even retrospectively, and store it with the project.
  • Storage. Primary data live on the Slurm cluster under /nfs/KODIAK2/PROBIO3/EVAL_SAMPLES/KIT_COMPLEXITY/. Confirm that system is an approved environment for this data classification, and that the working copies on your laptop are covered too. Sensitive human sequence data should not be sitting only on personal machines or in consumer cloud storage.
  • Retention. KI is a public authority, so research material is subject to the Swedish Archives Act and to KI's own rule on preservation of research material, which sets a minimum retention period (commonly stated as at least ten years after completion; confirm the current wording and registration number). Decide now who owns the HPC directory after your defence and write it into the archive README.
  • The MEB PhD archive template is not filled in. Readme.txt at the top level is still the blank form. This is your department's own standard and it is the single most visible unmet requirement in the repository. Fill it in, including the location of the primary data, the software environment, and the person responsible after you leave.

Reproducibility and provenance

KI's guidance on good research practice expects that published results can be traced back to the data and code that produced them. The current state does not meet that bar, for reasons already documented elsewhere on this site.

Data sharing and the data availability statement

Raw FASTQ and BAM files from ProBio participants and patient FFPE material cannot be deposited in an open repository. The usual route for Swedish projects is controlled access through the Swedish federated node of the European Genome-phenome Archive, with a metadata description registered so the dataset is findable. Points to settle:

  • Whether the ethics permit and consent allow deposition at all, and under what access conditions.
  • Transfers outside the EU and EEA need a legal basis, which is the reason the Swedish node is normally preferred over depositing directly abroad. Confirm current practice with KI's research data support.
  • Register a dataset description via the Swedish National Data Service so the study is discoverable even where the data themselves are restricted.
  • Derived, non-identifying summary data can be shared openly. The per-library metric tables in manuscript/data/ are a good candidate and would let others reproduce every figure in the paper without any access request.

Suggested split

Deposit the summary tables and all analysis code openly, and place only the read-level data under controlled access. That satisfies the journal, satisfies open-science expectations, and keeps the sensitive layer protected. It also happens to be the split this repository is already organised around.

Authorship, contributions and conflicts of interest

  • Authorship criteria. KI follows the ICMJE recommendations. Every listed author must meet all four criteria, and everyone who meets them must be listed. Apply this deliberately to the two operators whose laboratory work the study rests on.
  • "The ProBio team" in the author list. A group name in the byline is acceptable under ICMJE only when the individual members who meet the authorship criteria are named, usually in a supplementary list. If the members do not meet the criteria, the group belongs in Acknowledgments. The AI review suggested moving it; the ICMJE rule is the reason to decide one way or the other.
  • Contributorship statement. Consider CRediT roles. This study has a clean division between laboratory work, pipeline development and analysis, and stating it also documents the operator split you are already disclosing as a limitation.
  • Conflicts of interest. This is a benchmark of seven commercial products, so the disclosure has to be explicit: whether any kits, reagents or sequencing capacity were provided free or at a discount by a manufacturer, whether any manufacturer had any role in design, analysis or the decision to publish, and any consulting or advisory relationships. State this even if the answer is "none", because a reader will assume the question was asked.
  • Infrastructure acknowledgment. Sequencing and the production environment referenced by your supervisor sit with Clinical Genomics and SciLifeLab. Check their expected acknowledgment wording and whether any staff meet authorship criteria.
  • Affiliation and ORCID. Use KI's prescribed affiliation format, and supply ORCID iDs for all KI authors. The draft's open affiliations 5 to 7 need resolving.

Publication and open access

  • KI expects publications to be openly available. Confirm whether Clinical Chemistry is covered by a national agreement through the Bibsam consortium, because that determines the article processing charge and the licence. Do this before submission, not after acceptance.
  • Register the publication in KI's publication database once accepted, and deposit the full text as required.
  • Check the EQUATOR network for an applicable reporting guideline. No checklist fits a library preparation benchmark exactly, but the journal has its own requirements for reporting analytical method comparisons, and the minimum-information conventions for sequencing experiments are worth following for the Methods.

Disclosure of generative AI use

Hazard 1, removed 20 Aug 2026 — the fabricated numbers are gone

Two AI-generated editorial critiques used to sit in the same folder as the real data, and both contained invented conversion rates formatted as if they were results. The risk was that one of those numbers reached the manuscript, which would be a fabrication finding regardless of intent. Both files have been deleted from the repository. Their structural advice, which was the useful part, is already absorbed into this review. Earlier revisions remain in the git history, so if either is ever recovered, treat every number in it as invented.

Hazard 2, still open — the disclosure statement

Most journals now require a statement of how generative AI was used in preparing a manuscript, and AI tools cannot be authors. Draft that disclosure now, against the journal's current policy and KI's guidance on generative AI in research. It has to cover this review and the deleted critiques, not only text that survives into the submitted manuscript.
