Design and statistics review

A reconstruction of the experimental layout as it was actually executed, a quantitative diagnosis of the pseudoreplication problem in the current figures, and a recommendation on the statistical analysis that keeps the presentation you already have.

The short version

ST is right about the unit of analysis, but the naive implementation would destroy the paper. The three technical replicates inside a batch are not independent (measured ICC is 0.46 on average, and reaches 0.98 for KAPAON in cfDNA), so the current Kruskal–Wallis + Dunn on nine libraries per kit is anticonservative. But collapsing to three points and rerunning a rank test cannot work: a Wilcoxon/Dunn comparison of 3 vs 3 has a minimum attainable p of 0.1 before any multiplicity correction. It is not conservative, it is structurally incapable of producing a significant pairwise result.

The fix is to aggregate to the batch and pool the batch-to-batch variance across the five or six kits within a sample type. That takes the error degrees of freedom from 4 to 10–12 and recovers most of the lost power, while honouring "three points become one point" exactly.

lmerTest::lmer(logit(y) ~ kit + (1 | kit:batch), REML = TRUE)
anova(m, ddf = "Kenward-Roger")   # + Holm-adjusted pairwise contrasts on batch means

Four things in the layout that constrain the analysis

Decision 1, resolved 17 Aug 2026 — the EVO cfDNA run

The January/February 2025 preparation is the one that goes into the paper. The June 2025 re-prep is excluded because it was carried out with documented protocol errors in the laboratory, making it a failed preparation rather than a competing estimate. This is what the plotting scripts already do, so no number on this site changes: cfDNA omnibus p = 0.0011, EVO 0.421. What remains is to write the reason down, since it currently exists only as spoken lab knowledge. Detail →

Constraint 2 — operator is completely aliased with kit

CDM prepared EVO and WM; VK prepared KAPA15, KAPAON, KAPAON-COV, IL and TWIST. No model can estimate or adjust for an operator effect. The assumption is untestable inside this dataset, so put the CG production VK-vs-CDM data in the supplement pre-emptively. Detail →

Constraint 3 — "batch" does not mean the same thing for every kit

WM's three batches in gDNA and both FFPE arms were all prepared on 2025-04-02 on three plates. WM cfDNA batches 1–2 share a day; EVO cfDNA batches 2–3 share a day. WM's apparent reproducibility advantage is partly structural. TWIST (1.9 %) and IL (2.3 %) achieved lower batch CV across three genuinely separate days. Detail →

Constraint 4 — adapter:insert ratio was not held constant

EVO swings ~6× across batches in FFPE and gDNA (≈100 / ≈57 / ≈370); every other kit holds within 1.1×. Part of EVO's batch instability is adapter titration, not chemistry. Detail →

One result that came out well — use it

The enzymatic-vs-sonication comparison is confounded with prep day in gDNA, which is exactly where the largest effect in the study sits (0.204 vs 0.039). But in FFPE the two arms share prep days and show the same direction. Use FFPE as the internal de-confounding replication; a statistical reviewer will otherwise flag the gDNA comparison. Detail →
