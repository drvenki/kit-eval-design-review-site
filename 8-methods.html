Statistical analysis

Each sample type was analysed separately. The experimental unit was the library-preparation batch: three independent preparations per kit, each comprising three technical replicates processed in parallel from the same DNA aliquot. Because technical replicates within a batch share operator, reagent lot, plate and hybridisation pool, they were not treated as independent observations. Conversion rate was logit-transformed and modelled as logit(y) ~ kit + (1 | batch within kit) using restricted maximum likelihood (lme4 1.1-x, R 4.x), with denominator degrees of freedom by the Kenward–Roger approximation (lmerTest). Pairwise kit contrasts were computed on the estimated batch-level means using the pooled batch-level standard deviation and adjusted for multiplicity by the Holm procedure within each sample type; effect sizes are reported as Hedges' g on the logit scale. Between-batch and within-batch standard deviations and the resulting intraclass correlation are reported for every kit × sample type combination (Supplementary Table Sx). Sensitivity analyses allowing kit-specific residual variances (nlme varIdent) and a rank-based test on batch means gave concordant conclusions. Library preparations were performed by two operators, with operator aliased with kit (EVO and WM by one operator, the remaining kits by the other); an operator effect therefore cannot be estimated from these data and is reported as a limitation, with operator-level reproducibility data from routine production shown in Supplementary Figure Sx.

Conversion rate definition

Conversion rate was defined as the number of unique molecules recovered at a fixed sequencing depth divided by the number of genome copies in the input. Unique-UMI target coverage was interpolated from a subsampling ladder at 3.5 × 107 read pairs per library, and divided by the theoretical maximum coverage, computed as the DNA input in nanograms divided by 3.218 pg per haploid genome copy [ref]. The measure is therefore unique-molecule recovery at a defined sequencing depth rather than an asymptotic saturation value; 35 M read pairs was chosen as [justification].

Experimental setup, closing the "Describe experimental setup" placeholder

Seven library-preparation kits were evaluated across four input conditions: plasma cfDNA at 30 ng, blood genomic DNA at 150 ng, and FFPE tumour DNA at 25 ng and 150 ng. Six kits were evaluated on cfDNA and five on genomic and FFPE DNA. One pooled DNA preparation was used per matrix: cfDNA from leftover plasma of several ProBio participants, genomic DNA from two blood donors, and FFPE DNA from punches of nine anonymised blocks aged one to four years, extracted individually and pooled. For each kit and input condition, three independent preparation batches were performed on separate occasions, each with three technical replicates, giving nine libraries per kit and input condition (n = 8 in six instances, see Table 1). All libraries were captured with the same ProBio panel using Twist Target Enrichment Standard Hybridisation v1 by a single operator, in hybridisation pools listed in Table 1, and sequenced on a NovaSeq X across three flow cells with libraries distributed across lanes. All libraries were processed through one bioinformatic pipeline and downsampled to 35 M read pairs before metric collection.

Adapter:insert ratio, closing the second placeholder

Adapter:insert molar ratios and PCR cycle numbers followed each manufacturer's recommendation for the given input amount and were therefore not equalised across kits (Table 1). For one kit (EVO) the adapter:insert ratio varied up to 6.3-fold between batches within the genomic and FFPE arms; this is reported as a source of between-batch variance for that kit (Supplementary Figure Sx).

Limitations, for the Discussion

This study has several limitations. First, replication was technical rather than biological: a single pooled DNA preparation per matrix type was used throughout, so the estimates do not cover donor-to-donor or block-to-block variability, and the FFPE results cannot be generalised across fixation protocols. Second, library preparations were performed by two operators with operator aliased with kit, so an operator effect cannot be separated from a kit effect. Third, adapter:insert ratio and PCR cycle number followed manufacturer recommendations and therefore differed between kits, so part of the observed differences reflects protocol rather than chemistry. Fourth, for one kit the three preparation batches were performed on the same day on separate plates, so its between-batch variance reflects plate-to-plate rather than day-to-day variation and is not directly comparable with that of the other kits. Fifth, the comparison of enzymatic against sonication-based fragmentation was performed on different days in the genomic DNA arm; it is supported by the FFPE arms, where both fragmentation methods shared preparation days. Finally, all conclusions are conditional on a single capture panel, hybridisation chemistry and analysis pipeline, including duplex consensus calling requiring single-strand agreement, which may penalise kits producing shorter inserts or different UMI structures.
