The endpoint, the exclusions, and what the results support

Everything outside the statistical model: how conversion rate is defined, which libraries went missing, what the corrected analysis actually licenses you to claim, and where the draft stands against the journal's requirements.

The conversion-rate endpoint itself

Defined in program/scripts/plot_QC_adapter_ratio.R, around lines 320 to 350:

theoretical_max_cov = input_ng × 1000 / 3.218          # 3.218 pg per haploid genome copy
saturation_cov      = approxfun(totalReadPairs, cov_unique_umi)(3.5e7)
conversion_rate     = saturation_cov / theoretical_max_cov

Points a reviewer will raise. None are fatal, but each needs a sentence in the Methods.

  • It is not a saturation value. It is the unique-UMI coverage at a fixed sequencing depth of 35 M read pairs, read off the subsampling ladder by linear interpolation. Kits that saturate at different depths are therefore compared at a single arbitrary point on their curves, and linear interpolation on a concave curve biases the estimate slightly low. Either rename it (for example "unique-molecule recovery at 35 M read pairs") or justify 35 M as the clinically relevant depth.
  • input_amount is hard-coded as 30 in that snippet. Confirm the 150 ng and 25 ng runs used the correct denominator. A wrong denominator would rescale a whole panel.
  • 3.218 pg per haploid genome needs a citation.
  • It is a bounded proportion, which is the reason for the logit scale in the model.

Report the FFPE numbers honestly, they are the most interesting result

FFPE 150 ng conversion runs 0.019 to 0.050, meaning 95 to 98 % of input molecules are lost. That is the single most clinically consequential number in the paper, and the draft does not currently state it.

Sample handling and exclusions

Criterion 1, supplied 20 Aug 2026 — libraries that failed capture

Four libraries failed target capture and yielded essentially no reads. They are absent from the conversion-rate files and from the HsMetrics table, so no result was seen and then discarded: cfDNA KAPAON B3R2, cfDNA WM B2R3, gDNA KAPAON-COV B3R1 and FFPE 25 ng KAPAON-COV B2R3. This is a defensible QC exclusion on a pre-specified quality ground, and it accounts for every group where n = 8 rather than 9, which is four of the 21 kit × sample-type groups. What still has to be written down is the threshold. State the read count, or the on-target or coverage criterion, below which a library was declared a capture failure, so a reader can check the rule rather than take the list on trust.

Criterion 2, still open — three EVO libraries dropped by name

plot_saturation_curves_20260512.R drops five libraries by name before plotting. Two of those filters are redundant, because KAPAON30ngB3R2 and KAPAONCOVARIS25ngB2R3 are capture failures that were never in the data. The other three are libraries that do carry results and are removed anyway: EVO batch 1 replicate 1 in gDNA, in FFPE 150 ng and in FFPE 25 ng. Removal by name, after seeing the data, with no stated criterion, is the one thing here a reviewer can attack directly. The pattern is at least suggestive rather than arbitrary, since all three are the same batch and replicate of the same kit, so the likely honest answer is that something went wrong with that preparation. Either restore them, or say what the shared cause was and fold it into criterion 1. Note that this affects only the saturation-curve figure: the conversion-rate analysis and Table 1 already include all three.

  • The two cfDNA filters applied in every script (dropping EVO.*-KH20250815 and IL.*-KH20250512) and the chrX drop for cfDNA in the GC-bias script must all appear in the Methods with their justification. The EVO one is now decided and justified; the IL re-hybridisation still needs its reason stated.
  • The FFPE material is a pool of nine blocks aged 1 to 4 years. Fixation history and block age are the dominant drivers of FFPE library yield; pooling averages them, but it also means the FFPE result cannot be generalised across fixation protocols. One sentence in Limitations.
  • No randomisation of plate position or index assignment is recorded, and there was no blinding. That is acceptable for a chemistry benchmark, but say so rather than leave it unstated.

What the corrected analysis actually supports

cfDNA 30 ng

EVO (0.421) ≈ WM (0.409) > IL (0.357) ≈ TWIST (0.356) > KAPAON (0.290) ≈ KAPA15 (0.242). Six of fifteen contrasts survive Holm. The defensible claim is that the newer chemistries (EVO, WM) outperform the legacy KAPA HyperPrep by roughly 1.7-fold, and that IL and TWIST are intermediate and the most batch-stable. This uses the January/February EVO preparation; the June re-prep is excluded as a failed preparation.

gDNA 150 ng

WM (0.219) ≈ KAPAON (0.204) > EVO (0.171) > KAPA15 (0.143) ≫ KAPAON-COV (0.039). Seven of ten contrasts. The enzymatic-versus-sonication difference is the largest effect in the study. State that it is confounded with prep day in gDNA and that it is supported by the day-blocked FFPE comparison.

FFPE 150 ng

KAPAON (0.050) > WM (0.033) ≈ KAPA15 (0.032) > KAPAON-COV (0.024) ≈ EVO (0.019). Eight of ten contrasts. The most robust panel in the study.

FFPE 25 ng

Omnibus p = 0.031, and no pairwise contrast survives correction. KAPAON has the highest mean (0.124) and the ordering matches FFPE 150 ng, but the batch variance is too large to support a pairwise claim.

Interpretation caveats that belong in the Discussion

  1. Conclusions are conditional on one DNA pool per matrix type, one capture panel (ProBio SNV/indel S3), one hybridisation chemistry (Twist v1) and one bioinformatic pipeline including duplex consensus calling with --require-single-strand-agreement. A kit that produces shorter inserts or a different UMI structure is penalised by that filter in ways that are not intrinsic to the chemistry.
  2. Adapter:insert ratio and PCR cycle number were not equalised across kits, so part of every between-kit difference is protocol rather than chemistry.
  3. Operator is aliased with kit: the EVO and WM results carry CDM's hands, the rest carry VK's.
  4. WM's apparent batch stability is inflated by same-day batches.
  5. Frame the recommendation in cost-per-usable-molecule or per-sample terms if you want the paper to be actionable clinically. A 1.7-fold conversion difference at 30 ng cfDNA translates directly into limit of detection for ctDNA, which is the reason a Clinical Chemistry reader cares.
